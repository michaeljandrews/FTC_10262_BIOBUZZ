Physics curve: t = k / √(W − W0)

Treats the HIVE as a rigid see-saw turning under a constant net torque. The load in the cell pushes with a torque proportional to its weight, and pivot friction resists with a roughly constant torque, equal to a load W0. That gives a constant angular acceleration, and from rest the swing angle grows with t², so the time to sweep the full angle is k / √(W − W0). k lumps together the swing angle, the arm length and the moment of inertia.

Fitted to three timed swings from your see-saw tests: 0.438 lb in 3.773 s, 0.440 lb in 4.700 s and 0.546 lb in 2.796 s (top to bottom). Loads of 0.364–0.385 lb did not move it, which set the 0.438 lb tip threshold.

k and W0 were chosen by non-linear least squares (minimizing the squared timing errors): k = 1.244 and W0 = 0.352 lb, R² ≈ 0.74. W0 comes out below the 0.438 lb threshold because it takes more force to start the swing (static friction) than to keep it going. This curve is the safer one for loads above the tested range.

Most of the remaining error comes from the two near-threshold runs, which differ by 0.93 s for a 0.002 lb change. Timing more runs, especially at 0.60–0.65 lb where the two equations disagree most, will improve both. Change the numbers here if you refit them.

A purely empirical fit with no physical model behind it.

Fitted to three timed swings from your see-saw tests: 0.438 lb in 3.773 s, 0.440 lb in 4.700 s and 0.546 lb in 2.796 s (top to bottom). Loads of 0.364–0.385 lb did not move it, which set the 0.438 lb tip threshold.

a and b were chosen by ordinary least squares: a = 10.087 s and b = 13.338 s per lb, R² ≈ 0.75, so each extra 0.01 lb takes about 0.13 s off the swing. It would reach zero seconds near 0.76 lb, so don't rely on it far above 0.546 lb; the planner never lets it go below 0.5 s.

Plan setup

Analysis options choose which parts of the plan to include: errors and recovery, the opponent, and whether each robot uses one collect time or a time per round.

A planning file holds every Planning input: robot settings, errors and recovery, the opponent, and the collect time for each round. It also holds the Field Settings, since the plan depends on them.

Save one per robot design or event to compare plans, or share it with your team. Loading a file replaces all current Planning inputs and Field Settings.

Robot settings

Each card describes one robot. The planner simulates both robots with these settings for every strategy.

Times. Launch time is the time per ball, or per volley when the robot launches all at once. AUTO: drive to launch spot is how long the robot takes to reach its launch spot with its 4 preloaded POLLEN. Time to drive to PARK is how early the robot stops to park before the end of AUTO or the end of the match.

Collects and Launches. Choose POLLEN only, NECTAR only or both. A robot only collects balls it can launch. On demand: the robot launches whichever ball it holds that finishes the tip with the least extra weight, otherwise its heaviest. FIFO: balls go in the order collected, NECTAR first.

Launch style. One at a time launches ball by ball. All at once launches everything the robot holds in one volley taking one launch time, so any weight above the threshold is dumped with the cell.

Holds. The most of each type the robot can carry, never more than 4 balls in total (G407). A POLLEN limit below 4 drops the extra preloaded POLLEN at the start, and a robot that can't use POLLEN drops all 4.

Fill to capacity when idle. While the HIVE is swinging, a robot that still has empty slots goes for more balls instead of waiting. A top-up trip takes the driving part plus a share of the search time for each empty slot. At the end of AUTO, a robot set to park uses any spare time to collect POLLEN from your GARDEN or your FLOWERS, then parks and keeps them for TELEOP.

Park after AUTO / Park at end. 5 points each per robot when it reaches the parking area in time.

FLOWERS and GARDENS. Every robot that takes in POLLEN can collect from a GARDEN at its normal collect time. Can collect from FLOWERS lets it also take POLLEN out of the bottom of a FLOWER, using FLOWER collection time for 4 balls (a share of it for fewer). A NECTAR in the bottom spot blocks the FLOWER. Only one robot can use a GARDEN or FLOWER at a time. Analysis options set which source robots try first and whether they may use the opponent's FLOWERS (TELEOP only).

Last 60 s. Keep tipping the HIVE, fill your FLOWERS, or drop balls in your GARDEN. A robot filling FLOWERS puts NECTAR on top so you own them, and goes back to the HIVE once they are full. Dropping in the GARDEN scores only 1 point a ball.

Strategy comparison

Sorted by points, highest first. Letters follow the ranking and change when the order does.

Points include HIVE, LEAVE, PARK, FLOWER and GARDEN points.

Each FLOWER holds up to 6 balls and starts with 4 POLLEN. Balls come out only at the bottom (everything above drops down one spot) and go in only at the top (falling to the lowest open spot). A NECTAR in the bottom spot can't be removed, so nothing more can be taken from that FLOWER, though balls can still be added while there is room. FLOWER points count every ball in the FLOWER, including the starting POLLEN.

FLOWERS: the alliance with the top-most NECTAR of its color in a FLOWER scores 2 points for every ball in its scoring volume (up to the FLOWER scoring capacity in Field Settings), and the alliance with the lowest NECTAR there gets a 5-point bottom NECTAR bonus. NECTAR may only enter FLOWERS in the last 60 s (G410). GARDENS: every ball in a GARDEN at the end scores 1 point for that GARDEN's color, including the 4 starting POLLEN, so taking POLLEN out of a GARDEN costs that alliance a point each.

Balls left in the HIVE: each POLLEN or NECTAR still in your upward-facing cell when everything comes to rest after the buzzer scores 2 points. Balls in the cell at the end of AUTO score nothing (Table 10-2).

How the simulation works

  • Match clock: AUTO 0–30 s, 8 s transition (robots stop, the HIVE keeps swinging), TELEOP 38–158 s.
  • Each robot starts with 4 POLLEN. The upward cell starts with 3 NECTAR (0.273 lb).
  • Robots launch one ball at a time. The HIVE tips the moment the cell reaches the threshold. A robot keeps any balls it has left and launches them into the new cell once the swing finishes. Nobody can launch while the HIVE is swinging.
  • When the HIVE tips, every ball in the cell lands on the floor. Your human player then brings in one NECTAR (5 staged). G427 releases all remaining staged NECTAR with 60 s left. NECTAR from a dumped cell goes back into the pool robots can collect.
  • If a strategy calls for NECTAR and none is available, the robot collects POLLEN instead.
  • Collect shortfall: the robot returns with fewer than 4 balls at the rate you set, spread evenly across rounds (at 10%, one ball short every 2–3 rounds). The missing balls are POLLEN, since your human player places NECTAR in a known spot. Preloads are never short.
  • Launch misses: the set share of launches miss the cell, spread evenly (at 10%, every 10th ball). A missed ball still takes a full launch time and lands on the floor. A missed NECTAR goes back into the collectable pool.
  • A launch has to finish before the robot leaves to PARK, or before 158 s. A swing still in progress at the buzzer counts. Balls left in the upward cell at the end score 2 each.
  • Early return: each collect round is driving out, finding balls one at a time at an even pace, then driving back. When the cell is partly full but short of tipping, a collecting robot turns back as soon as it holds enough balls to finish the tip, unless its partner can already finish it. In endgame mode this only happens inside the window you set. With early return on, a robot also cuts its last round short so it can launch before it leaves to PARK.
  • Your HIVE's two cells: Hive 1 is the target at the start (facing up, holding 3 NECTAR). When it tips, Hive 2 faces up and becomes the target, and so on. Odd-numbered tips empty Hive 1 and even-numbered tips empty Hive 2. The HIVE lane shows H1 or H2 for whichever cell faces up between tips.
  • Robot capabilities override the strategy's ball mix. A POLLEN-only robot never carries NECTAR, and a NECTAR-only robot carries as many NECTAR as are available (up to 4), so it can come back with none. The opponent uses the same capabilities as your robots.
  • Ball counts track all 40 POLLEN and both alliances' 8 NECTAR. The opponent runs the strategy you choose, at the cycle time you set, with your failure rates. In AUTO, robots only use their preloads and balls from known places: their own GARDEN and, if allowed, their own FLOWERS. They never pick up loose balls or NECTAR from the floor, and never touch the opponent's side (G402). In TELEOP they use both GARDENS, loose balls on the floor and FLOWERS, in the order set in Analysis options. Hover anywhere on a timeline to see the count at that moment.
  • Scoring for movement (Section 10): LEAVE 3 points per robot (assumed for both), AUTO PARK 5 points per robot in the LOADING ZONE at the end of AUTO, and PARK 5 points per robot in the LOADING ZONE at the end of the match. A robot set to park after AUTO stops what it is doing in time to drive to the LOADING ZONE by 30 s; if it is still carrying balls, it drives back for the same time when TELEOP starts. SWARM needs 16 points from LEAVE + AUTO PARK + PARK.

Log an event

Log what each robot does, in any order. The log sorts itself by time.

Leaves to collect: the robot leaves its launch spot. At launch spot: it is back and ready to launch, with the balls it picked up on that trip (choose 0 and 0 when it arrives with only its preload). Launches: one launch, or a volley of several balls of the same type, and how many missed the cell. HIVE tips: the alliance's HIVE starts to swing.

Collect times, launch times and error rates are worked out from these events using the same rules as the Planning tab.

Collect time: from leaving to collect until back at the launch spot, not counting the 8 s transition. Collect shortfall: balls picked up compared with 4 per trip. Launch time: from arriving (or the previous launch, or the HIVE finishing its swing) to the launch; the median is shown. Launch miss rate: missed balls out of all balls launched.

Tip weight is the weight of the balls recorded as scoring in the cell when the HIVE tipped. Swing times come from the swing-time model in Field Settings.
